    Why go

    A country that
    said no to more

    Bhutan only opened to foreign tourists in 1974, and it still asks every visitor to pay into its future. In return you get monasteries that are lived in, forests that are protected by law and trails you share with yak herders instead of crowds.

    70%+

    Still forest

    The constitution requires at least 60% of the land to stay forested forever. Today it is over 70%.

    1st

    Carbon negative

    Its forests absorb more CO₂ than the whole country emits, and its rivers power homes with hydroelectricity.

    0

    Traffic lights in the capital

    Thimphu tried one and residents found it impersonal. White-gloved police still direct traffic by hand.

    7,570m

    Never summited

    Gangkhar Puensum is the highest unclimbed mountain on Earth. Bhutan has banned mountaineering since 2003 out of respect for the gods who live on its peaks.

    1974

    Late to tourism

    The first foreign tourists arrived for the coronation of the fourth king. The country has guarded its culture carefully ever since.

    $100/night

    High value, low volume

    The Sustainable Development Fee pays for free healthcare, education and conservation. It also keeps the trails quiet.

    Iconic mountains

    Peaks no one
    has stood on

    Bhutan's northern border is a wall of 7,000-metre summits that the country chooses to leave alone. You can still walk beneath them: the Jomolhari trek reaches its base camp at 4,080 m, and the Snowman Trek crosses passes above 5,000 m.

    Iconic skies

    Skies that
    carry prayers

    Every ridge in Bhutan is strung with lungta, “wind horses”: prayer flags in blue, white, red, green and yellow. The belief is simple. Each time the wind moves a flag, it carries the prayer printed on it out across the valley.

    Paro approach · 2,235 m

    The most dramatic landing in the world

    Planes bank between ridges and only see the runway moments before touchdown. Landings are flown by hand in daylight, and only a few dozen pilots are certified to make them. Ask for a window seat.

    Dochula · 3,100 m · Oct–Feb

    Sunrise over the whole Himalaya

    On clear winter mornings the pass shows the full Bhutan range, Gangkhar Puensum included, glowing pink above 108 white chortens.

    Druk · the Thunder Dragon

    A country named after a storm

    Monsoon thunder rolling through the valleys was heard as the voice of a dragon. The dragon now sits on the national flag, holding jewels for wealth and protection.

    When to go

    Every month
    has a reason

    Masked dances in dzong courtyards, cranes returning from Tibet, rhododendron forests flowering red. Pick a month to see what is happening. Festival dates follow the lunar calendar, so confirm them before you book.

    Nights in Bhutan
    7

    Covers government fees only. Hotels, a licensed guide, a driver and meals are booked separately, usually through a Bhutanese tour operator or your hotel. SDF rates are set at USD 100 per night until 31 August 2027.

    By air

    Fly into Paro

    Drukair and Bhutan Airlines fly to Paro from cities including Delhi, Kolkata, Kathmandu, Bangkok, Singapore and Dhaka. Flying from Kathmandu, sit on the left for Everest.

    By land

    Cross from India

    Overland entry is through Phuentsholing, Gelephu or Samdrup Jongkhar. The drive from Phuentsholing to Thimphu climbs from the plains into pine forest in about six hours.

    Paperwork

    Visa and guide

    Apply for the visa online before you travel (USD 40). International visitors need a licensed guide for most travel beyond Thimphu and Paro. Start at bhutan.travel.
